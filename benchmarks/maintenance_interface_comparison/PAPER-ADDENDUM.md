# Proposed paper addendum: exploratory interface comparison

This text is supplied for review and is not inserted into the frozen manuscript.

We additionally attempted a preregistered exploratory comparison of Native
semantic tools and a controlled source-editing workbench on six previously
selected ALVA maintenance tasks. Both conditions used fresh sessions of the
same requested model/effort, original programs, documentation, finite tests and
resource thresholds. Two repetitions per condition scheduled 24 turn requests.
This evaluates a tool bundle on known ALVA components, not the comparative
utility of ALVA and mainstream languages.

Provider quota interrupted the twenty-second request. The reused controller
failed to classify that terminal error as a common fault and submitted two
further scheduled requests, both with zero tool activity. These three outcomes
are unavailable for efficacy inference; they are not ordinary model failures.
Post-run CLI resolution also differed from the initially pinned binary hash,
with no per-slot executable stamps to resolve whether a change occurred during
execution. The primary disposition is INCONCLUSIVE_PROVIDER_LIMIT, with this
additional identity limitation and an explicit protocol deviation.

Ten pairs are unaffected by quota. Descriptively, source editing completed
nine and Native eight. Among eight pairs where both completed, median reported
cumulative total tokens were 157,012.5 and 459,289 respectively; cached input
remains included. These selected survivor costs are not dollar costs or an
unconditional efficiency estimate. The observations provide no favorable
Native pattern on the accessible pairs and do not support a general advantage
claim. They motivate attention to interface overhead and evaluation infrastructure,
without revising the paper's frozen historical outcomes or establishing
statistical inferiority of semantic editing.

Supporting artifacts: PROTOCOL.md, FREEZE.json, RESULTS.json, VALIDATION.json,
ATTEMPTS.md and REPORT.md in this directory. This addendum involves new model
turn requests; the original manuscript's zero-new-experiment drafting statement
must not be extended to cover it.
